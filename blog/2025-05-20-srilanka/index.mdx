Sri Lanka (known as the "__pearl of the Indian Ocean__") is one of those places that rewards slow travel. Lush jungles, ancient temples, wildlife, coastline, and a culture that feels genuinely warm. This journal covers our south-west route, shot over three weeks with a Sony a6400 and a DJI Mini Pro 4.

![Sri Lanka blog header image of elephant](_BlogHeader.jpg)

This post covers practical travel information for the south-west circuit, followed by location-by-location photography. If you're here for the images, [**skip straight to photography**](#photography).


## General Information

**Climate**: 

- Sri Lanka sits just north of the equator. Coastal regions experience 25–35°C; the inland highlands are noticeably cooler at 15–18°C.
- Monsoon seasons split the island: **November → March** is dry in the south and south-west, wet in the north-east. **April → September** reverses this. Plan your route accordingly.

**Hygiene**:

- Tap water is not safe to drink! Use bottled water and check the seal on every bottle. Be cautious with ice.
- Mosquitoes are prevalent, particularly inland and near rice fields. Pack a tropical repellent and check your country's vaccine recommendations before travel. Sun cream is essential.
- Street dogs are common throughout Sri Lanka. Avoid approaching them regardless of how friendly they appear.

**Language**: Sri Lanka has two official languages: Sinhala (predominant in the south and west) and Tamil (north and east). English is widely used in signage and tourist infrastructure but is not guaranteed with all locals.


## The South West Route

Our route followed one of the more popular circuits: Colombo → Dambulla → Kandy → Ella → Udawalawae → South Coast, returning north to Colombo along the western coastline. For three weeks this is a pretty reasonable balance of highland travel, wildlife, and coast without feeling rushed.


## Gear

**Backpack:** If you're moving between locations (even primarily by taxi or train) a backpack is the only practical choice. Buses in Sri Lanka will use every available space, and suitcases are genuinely difficult to manage on the back roads you will inevitably encounter. For camera gear, a backpack also offers better padding, better organisation, and handles rough terrain far better. I traveled with two: a main **travel pack** and a smaller **day pack** for shoots.

**Drone:** To fly a drone legally in Sri Lanka it must be registered with the Civil Aviation Authority before you travel. The process is straightforward via their online portal; for a DJI Mini Pro 4, my approval came through within one day of submitting. Two location-specific restrictions worth noting:

- **Ella:** *"You are not permitted to operate the drone underneath the Nine Arch Bridge due to provocation of Wasp."*
- **Kandy:** *"You are not permitted to operate the drone over Temple of the Tooth Relic Premises."*

<details>
    <summary>
    **More Info:** Step-by-step Drone Application
    </summary>
    1) Visit the CAA portal and submit your application: 🌐 https://portal.caa.lk/drone/apply-now/report.php.  
    2) Have to hand: your drone ID, insurance details, planned flight locations, and your accommodation address.  
    3) For flight times, entering standard daylight hours (e.g. 08:00–19:00) is sufficient if you don't have a specific shoot planned.  
    4) You'll receive a tracking ID and estimated processing time by email.  
    5) The application passes to security clearance; you'll be cc'd.  
    6) Once cleared, a payment link arrives to complete the registration.
</details>

**Clothing:** Sri Lanka's climate calls for lightweight, breathable clothing throughout. Pack a fleece for highland evenings (temperatures in Ella and the central mountains drop noticeably after dark), a lightweight rain jacket for the short torrential downpours that can appear even in the dry season, and both sturdy walking shoes and flip flops.


## Getting Around

**Tuk tuk:** The backbone of short-distance travel and the only way to reach many of the more remote spots. As a foreign traveller, you'll almost certainly be quoted above the local rate; ask your accommodation for a reference price before you leave, and use the PickMe app to cross-reference. PickMe (Sri Lanka's equivalent of Uber) is useful as a benchmark, though many drivers won't accept app bookings due to the 20% commission charged on smaller vehicles.

**Car:** For longer inter-city legs, or groups of three or more, a private car is often the more practical option. Ask your accommodation to recommend a local driver, which typically works out better value than app-based services for distance travel, and the drivers often double as informal guides.

**Bus:** Sri Lanka's buses are fast, frequent, and an experience in themselves: packed, loud, and seemingly indifferent to road geometry. They're also the cheapest way to cover ground along major routes; highly recommended for longer stretches where the schedule is flexible.

**Train:** We took the train twice: the famous Kandy to Ella route through the tea highlands, and a second journey up the west coast back to Colombo.

## Photography

### Dambulla

From Colombo we took a tuk tuk to Negombo, then the bus south via **No. 34** (Negombo → Kurunegala), changing to **No. 48** (Kurunegala → Dambulla), with a final tuk tuk from the bus station. We stayed at [__Blue Mountains View__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g12897977-d12605839-Reviews-Blue_Mountains_View-Lenadora_Central_Province.html), a homestay roughly 21 km south of the main city on a working farm growing fruit, vegetables, and spices. Host Pradeep runs the tourist side alongside his father (who manages the farming) and his mother (who handles the cooking), serving home-grown food in the traditional style. The morning farm tour was one of the highlights of the entire trip: bananas, tomatoes, coconuts, spices, and a window into daily rural life that you simply don't get from a hotel. Rooms and communal areas were well kept, and the seating areas near the kitchen are a good spot for watching the wildlife come and go.

The main draw for sightseeing is [__Sigiriya__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attractions-g304141-Activities-Sigiriya_Central_Province.html) (Lion's Rock), the ancient rock fortress rising 200 metres above the jungle and one of the most striking aerial subjects in the country. [__Pidurangala__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304141-d3220900-Reviews-Pidurangala_Rock-Sigiriya_Central_Province.html) is worth combining on the same day for the view back across Lion's Rock. The [__Dambulla Cave Temple__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g665220-d12921436-Reviews-Dambulla_Cave_Temple-Habarana_North_Central_Province.html) and the [__Economic Food Market__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304133-d13414363-Reviews-Dambulla_Dedicated_Economic_Centre-Dambulla_Central_Province.html) round out the area if you have the time.

### Kandy

Most southbound buses from Dambulla pass through Kandy; we boarded along the Kandy Highway and took a tuk tuk from the main bus stop to [__The Bunkalow__](https://www.booking.com/hotel/lk/bunkalow.en-gb.html), a quieter base outside the city centre by the river with both private rooms and hostel bunks available.

The standout visit outside the city is [__Ambuluwawa Tower__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304138-d20314343-Reviews-Ambuluwawa_Tower-Kandy_Kandy_District_Central_Province.html), a spiralling concrete tower at the peak of a biosphere reserve with a 360° view across the surrounding hills (the drone shot below was taken from above the treeline on the approach path). In the city itself, [__Kandy Market__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304138-d3634738-Reviews-Kandy_Market_Hall-Kandy_Kandy_District_Central_Province.html), the [__Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304138-d317476-Reviews-Temple_of_the_Sacred_Tooth_Relic-Kandy_Kandy_District_Central_Province.html), and the [__Kandy Spice Walk__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g304138-d25800444-Reviews-Kandy_Spice_Walk-Kandy_Kandy_District_Central_Province.html) are all worth your time. The nearby [__tea factories__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g5994634-d5993853-Reviews-Embilmeegama_Tea_Factory-Embilmeegame_Kandy_District_Central_Province.html) make a good half-day trip if the schedule allows.

### Kandy → Ella Train

:::tip Train Info

The train from Kandy to Ella is one of the most scenic rail journeys in the world, and worth treating as a destination in itself rather than just a transfer.

:::

Reservations are available but not essential. Unreserved 2nd and 3rd class tickets go on sale one hour before departure, so arrive 1.5–2 hours early to queue comfortably. Departing from Kandy, the train leaves from either platform 1 or 2 and can be boarded from both sides.

This is one of the most scenic train journeys you are likely to take: tea fields rolling across mountain ridges, mist in the valleys, and constantly changing light. The open-door carriage has become one of the most photographed moments on the entire route, and at busy times it can feel contrived. A window seat away from the crowd makes for a more considered and far more enjoyable journey.

### Ella

Two nights at [__Glorious Nature Inn__](https://www.booking.com/Share-TGKjSA), with a room looking directly over the sunrise breaking above the mountain line: one of the more memorable mornings of the trip. Most accommodation along Kitalella Road at sufficient elevation will offer a similar view.

The [__Nine Arches Bridge__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g616035-d4137010-Reviews-Nine_Arches_Bridge-Ella_Uva_Province.html) (built in 1919 without steel, due to wartime shortages) sits deep in jungle and frames exceptionally well in almost any light. [__Little Adams Peak__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g616035-d2478367-Reviews-Little_Adam_s_Peak_View_Point-Ella_Uva_Province.html) at sunrise is worth the early start for the aerial perspective alone. Ella Town itself is a pleasant spot for an evening wander.

### Udawalawae

Udawalawae is more remote than the other stops, and reaching it without losing half a day requires a private taxi or a carefully timed bus connection. We arranged a driver through our accommodation in Ella; he stopped en route at [__Ravana Falls__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g616035-d2255787-Reviews-Ravana_Ella_Falls-Ella_Uva_Province.html) and a roadside stand for king coconuts, coming to around 10,000 LKR (approximately 30 EUR) including stops. We stayed two nights at [__Walawa Reach Hotel__](https://www.booking.com/Share-lp18Cf), though one is probably sufficient: if you've booked a sunrise safari, you'll be back at the hotel by mid-morning with little reason to stay a second day.

The national park is home to one of the largest elephant populations in Sri Lanka. The [__safari__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionProductReview-g3577009-d23907829-Udawalawe_National_Park_Private_Safari_Tour-Udawalawa_Sabaragamuwa_Province.html) is best done at sunrise: the light is better for photography and the wildlife is considerably more active.

### South Coast

From Udawalawae we took a private taxi to Weligama (the bus doesn't run frequently enough to make it practical), then continued back to Colombo by bus along the coastline. We spent a week on the south coast, split between [__Lucky's Surf Camp__](https://www.booking.com/Share-Uytt2p) in Weligama and [__Hummus Hostel__](https://www.booking.com/Share-9qsVWZG) in Hikkaduwa. Lucky's was the standout: a relaxed atmosphere right on the water, with a [surf school on-site](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g612380-d12283647-Reviews-Lucky_s_Surf_School_Surf_Camp-Weligama_Matara_Southern_Province.html) and the kind of energy that makes it easy to stay longer than planned.

The south coast offers some of the best aerial photography of the trip. [__Coconut Tree Hill__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g1407334-d17469090-Reviews-Coconut_Tree_Hill-Mirissa_Southern_Province.html) at golden hour, [__Mirissa Beach__](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g1407334-d6104104-Reviews-Mirissa_Beach-Mirissa_Southern_Province.html), and the fishing boats off Weligama at first light are all strong subjects. The sunset shot below was taken from the drone just offshore from Weligama.
